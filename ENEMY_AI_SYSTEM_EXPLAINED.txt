RGB ENEMY AI SYSTEM - COMPLETE TECHNICAL AND DESIGN EXPLANATION
================================================================

Document purpose
----------------

Documentation reconciliation: 2026-10-22. The AI implementation description
below remains dated October 17; no new AI behavior was added in this update.
For the subsequent three-arena demo level and October 18 HUD fix, see
CHROMATIC_FOUNDRY_LEVEL.md and the latest section of CODEX_HANDOFF.txt.
The HUD now selects the player's current arena and displays zero between rooms;
this does not reset the arena's true completion or its AI pressure profiles.
The Unreal-MAP investigation and scope decision are already documented later
in this file. It was considered for the presentation, not integrated into RGB.

This document explains how the enemy AI currently implemented in RGB works. It
is intended to be a reliable technical reference and a base for a later AI
presentation. It describes the actual C++ implementation as of 2026-10-17,
including the shared enemy framework, arena-level multi-agent coordination,
ground enemies, teleporting enemies, flying enemies, attacks, damage,
respawning, editor configuration, and known limitations.

The important architectural idea is that the system has two decision levels:

1. The arena coordinator makes group-level decisions. It decides which enemies
   may attack, which ground enemy receives the close melee role, where pressure
   slots should be, and which special destination is reserved by which enemy.

2. Each enemy actor makes its own local decisions. It performs movement,
   collision checks, attacks, visual state changes, teleport selection, or
   flying steering according to its enemy type.

This is a lightweight multi-agent system. It is not implemented with Unreal
Behavior Trees, blackboards, EQS, or NavMesh pathfinding. The authoritative
rules are implemented directly in C++ using explicit state, timers, collision
queries, and short-lived coordination assignments.

ARENA-DRIVEN PRESSURE PROFILES (2026-10-17)
==========================================

Arena Control is the tuning authority for its area. Enable Use Arena Enemy
Tuning under RGB > Arena > AI. Ground Enemy Tuning, Teleporter Enemy Tuning,
Flying Enemy Tuning and Coordination Tuning then supply the settings for all
enemies owned by that arena. This option lets existing arenas adopt the profiles
individually. An enemy cannot be registered to two different arena owners.

Each progress value has At No Progress, Near Completion, and an optional
Response Curve. Completion is the true puzzle fraction from 0 to 1; hiding the
UI progress bar on camera-zone exit does not change this fraction. Without a
curve the values interpolate linearly. Curves map completion to a bounded blend
factor, allowing a gradual rise or escalation late in the encounter. Attributes
decrease again when platform progress is lost. At full completion the encounter
ends; endpoint values describe the limiting pressure just before success.

Per-type profiles replace the older shared movement multiplier when enabled,
so their values do not accidentally stack. Respawn delay keeps its separate
toggle. Runtime values are visible on enemies under Arena Attributes. Reset
and respawn reapply the current arena settings and retain authored enemy values.

Coordination
------------

Maximum Attackers is a progress value rounded down and clamped to zero or more.
The default 1-to-3 range permits one attacker below 50 percent, two from 50
percent and three at the endpoint. Adjust the endpoint or curve to admit more
attackers earlier. Decision Interval defaults to 0.2 seconds, with one second
of Minimum Role Commitment.

The coordinator filters active, living enemies for threat eligibility. Ground
enemies require ground, sight, similar height and a locally clear direction;
flyers require sight; teleporters require the same combat level, horizontal attack
range and no active teleport. Attacks already executing keep their acquired permissions. Ready-to-hit
enemies take priority over distant pursuers, followed by committed roles and
distance in X/Z (X only for teleporters). A decreasing budget lets existing
attacks finish while preventing new attacks above the new limit.

All three enemy types share this budget. Unselected ground enemies patrol.
Flyers continue tracking but need selection to attack. Teleport relocation is
independent of attack permission. Current Assignments on the EnemyCoordinator
component lists each enemy's role, attack eligibility, execution and movement.

Ground profile
--------------

- Patrol Speed Multiplier and Chase Speed Multiplier independently scale the
  enemy's authored Max Walk Speed; they are not multiplied by one another.
- Chase Preference (0..1) controls pursuit participation. Each random decision
  persists for a commitment interval. An enemy already in melee range can
  defend itself even with low chase preference.
- Detection Range and Attack Cooldown Multiplier can increase with progress.
- Patrol periodically chooses left or right using Minimum and Maximum Patrol
  Direction Time. Wall and ledge probes continue to reject unsafe movement.
- There is no separate preparation delay. The attack montage begins immediately.
  Attack Hit Delay is still the user's damage moment measured from animation
  start. Arena progress never changes that timing or montage playback rate.

Teleporter profile
------------------

- Fill Teleport Target Points on the Arena Control with placed Target Point
  actors. Enabled arena tuning uses this list instead of tag/radius discovery.
  Points must be inside that arena camera zone; invalid entries log a warning.
- Minimum and Maximum Idle Time blend with progress. A progress change refreshes
  an idle wait; active teleport charges and attacks retain their current timing.
- Danger Radius scores authored destinations around the player in X/Z. Selection
  blends relative proximity, closeness inside that radius and randomness, using
  Player Proximity Weight. Destinations must remain unoccupied and unreserved.
- The destination capsule must fit inside the camera zone. Points represent
  platform surfaces; the capsule half-height is added and Y remains constrained.
- Same Level Height Tolerance compares feet heights before attack eligibility
  (default 60 cm), rejecting players on a different floor. Once eligible,
  Horizontal Attack Range uses only X distance; vertical distance does not
  contribute to the coordinator's teleporter distance score.
- Damage is separately bounded by the authored AoE Radius in X/Z. At resolution
  both same-level/horizontal eligibility and the actual AoE radius are checked
  again. Trigger range does not enlarge the damage radius.
- Attack Cooldown Multiplier controls repetition separately from teleport idle
  time. Existing teleport charge effects and Niagara AoE warning timing remain.

Flying profile
--------------

- Speed Multiplier and Acceleration Multiplier scale the authored flight values.
- Player Tracking Weight, Enemy Separation Weight/Radius, Geometry Avoidance
  Weight and Geometry Probe Distance each have progress endpoints and a curve.
- Faster, tighter pursuit still uses swept collision and camera-zone confinement.
- Attack Cooldown Multiplier changes repetition; attack hit timing stays fixed.

Setup: enable Use Arena Enemy Tuning, assign the arena's Teleport Target Points,
and tune the profile endpoints. Keep each enemy in its owning arena's Assigned
Enemies list. Conflicting arena ownership is rejected with a warning.

The remaining sections explain the common execution systems. References to tag
discovery, shared speed scaling and ground pressure slots describe the legacy
mode used when Use Arena Enemy Tuning is disabled.

Finding damage that is difficult to see
---------------------------------------

All accepted player health damage now logs its source under LogRGBPlayerDamage,
including attacker name/class, source and player positions, horizontal/vertical
separation, damage amount and remaining health. RecieveDamage remains available
to Blueprints; unattributed calls are labeled accordingly. Last Damage Source
and Last Damage Amount are visible on the player instance during PIE.

Enter rgb.DebugPlayerDamage 1 in the PIE console or Output Log command field to
display the attacker name and draw a red line/sphere at the attacker for six
seconds after a hit. Use rgb.DebugPlayerDamage 0 to disable these temporary
visuals. Hidden or inactive enemies and obsolete attack callbacks cannot apply
damage. The corrected teleporter rule rejects attacks between different combat
levels and limits damage to AoE Radius. These diagnostics identify any remaining
damage source, including enemies off-screen or unattributed Blueprint calls.


1. MAIN CLASSES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
====================================

ARGBEnemyCharacter
------------------

This is the shared base class for enemies. It owns behavior that all enemy
types need:

- Enemy color and color-specific elimination.
- Health.
- Ground patrol and basic ground pursuit.
- Shared action-state reporting.
- Shared melee attack timing and player damage.
- Access to the arena coordinator.
- Access to the arena camera zone.
- Attack permission acquisition and release.
- Shared target reservation helper functions.
- Elimination notification.
- Supporting-platform color transfer for grounded enemies.

ARGBTeleporterEnemyCharacter
----------------------------

This derives from ARGBEnemyCharacter. It disables ordinary patrol movement and
adds:

- Random idle time between teleport attempts.
- Authored Target Point discovery.
- Weighted destination selection.
- Destination occupancy validation.
- Shared target-point reservations.
- Teleport charge timing.
- Niagara charge, departure, and arrival presentation hooks.
- A mesh implosion during the teleport charge.
- A radial area-of-effect attack with a warning period.

ARGBFlyingEnemyCharacter
------------------------

This also derives from ARGBEnemyCharacter. It disables ground patrol and adds:

- Continuous pursuit of the player's live position in the X/Z gameplay plane.
- Flying acceleration and deceleration.
- Swept collision movement.
- Local obstacle-direction sampling.
- Separation steering from other enemies.
- Pawn overlap behavior so flyers do not physically shove enemies or players.
- Camera-zone confinement.
- A flying-specific player attack-range test.
- An optional visual-facing mode.

URGBArenaEnemyCoordinatorComponent
----------------------------------

Every ARGBArenaControl owns one of these components. It is the group-level
multi-agent coordinator. Its responsibilities are:

- Registering enemies in one arena.
- Remembering each enemy's mobility type.
- Finding the player.
- Assigning one or more melee attackers.
- Assigning remaining enemies to pressure roles.
- Treating teleporters as special-role enemies.
- Limiting how many attacks may happen simultaneously.
- Giving assignments a minimum commitment time to reduce rapid role swapping.
- Giving assignments short leases so stale decisions expire.
- Reserving shared targets, such as teleporter Target Points.
- Releasing reservations and attack permissions when an enemy finishes,
  disappears, or is eliminated.

ARGBArenaControl
----------------

This actor owns the lifecycle around the AI:

- It holds the list of enemies assigned to the arena.
- It provides the arena coordinator to those enemies.
- It provides the arena camera zone to those enemies.
- It records the class and spawn transform of each enemy.
- It listens for elimination events.
- It unregisters eliminated enemies from coordination.
- It can respawn enemies after a configurable delay.
- It retains eliminated enemies as inactive instances and restores them at
  their recorded spawn transforms, preserving instance configuration.
- It reconnects respawned enemies to the coordinator and camera zone.
- It activates enemies only while the player is inside the arena camera zone.
- It removes enemies and cancels respawns when the arena is completed.
- It integrates enemy lifecycle with arena reset.

ARGBArenaCameraZone
-------------------

The camera zone is also used as an arena boundary for flying enemies. It can:

- Test whether a world position is inside the zone.
- Clamp a world position to the zone.
- Apply padding while clamping, allowing the flyer's entire collision capsule
  to remain inside instead of only keeping its center inside.


2. SHARED AI DATA
=================

Enemy mobility types
--------------------

ERGBEnemyMobilityType identifies the movement style used for coordination:

- Ground: ordinary platform-bound enemy.
- Flying: free movement in the side-view X/Z plane.
- Teleporter: stationary enemy that relocates between authored points.

The coordinator uses this type when constructing role positions. Ground
enemies keep their current Z height. Flying pressure slots may receive vertical
offsets. Teleporters are assigned the Special role.

Enemy coordination roles
------------------------

ERGBEnemyCoordinationRole contains:

- Unassigned: no active group role.
- MeleeAttacker: selected to occupy the close attack position and permitted to
  request an attack.
- Pressure: positioned farther from the player to prevent every enemy from
  stacking in the same location.
- Special: used by teleporter enemies. Their special behavior decides their
  exact teleport actions.

Coordination assignment
-----------------------

FRGBEnemyCoordinationAssignment is the message sent conceptually from the arena
coordinator to an enemy. It contains:

- Role: the selected coordination role.
- TargetLocation: the assigned world-space slot.
- bHasTargetLocation: whether the target is valid.
- bCanAttack: whether the enemy is currently eligible to request attack
  permission.
- SlotIndex: the pressure-slot index, or INDEX_NONE when no pressure index is
  used.
- LeaseExpiresAt: the world time at which this assignment becomes stale.

The lease matters because enemies must not obey an assignment forever if the
coordinator stops updating. GetAssignment returns false after the lease expires.


3. SHARED ACTION STATES
=======================

ERGBEnemyActionState reports what an enemy is currently doing. This provides a
common vocabulary for debugging and presentation even though different enemy
types execute different logic.

Idle
    The enemy is waiting or currently has no movement action to perform.

Patrol
    A ground enemy is moving through its ordinary patrol behavior.

Chase
    An enemy is pursuing the player or moving toward a combat assignment.

Turning
    A ground enemy is playing or simulating its direction-change sequence.

Attacking
    The attack animation is playing. Damage occurs at AttackHitDelay after it
    starts. The older AttackWindup enum entry remains for asset compatibility.

AttackCooldown
    The previous attack has finished and the enemy must wait before beginning
    another.

TeleportCharge
    A teleporter has selected and reserved a destination and is charging before
    moving.

AoEWindup
    A teleporter is warning the player before resolving radial damage.

Disabled
    The enemy has been eliminated or removed because the arena completed.

This enum is exposed to Blueprints for read-only presentation and diagnostics.
The authoritative behavior remains in C++.


4. ARENA-LEVEL MULTI-AGENT COORDINATION
=======================================

Registration
------------

At arena startup, ARGBArenaControl records each assigned enemy and registers it
with URGBArenaEnemyCoordinatorComponent. Dynamically spawned enemies inside the
camera zone are also detected and registered. A respawned enemy is registered
again after creation.

During registration, the arena gives the enemy:

- A pointer to the arena coordinator.
- A pointer to the arena camera zone.
- An elimination-event connection.
- A spawn-record index used by the respawn system.

Arena activation gate
---------------------

Arena-controlled enemies begin in Idle and remain inactive until the player
enters that arena's camera zone. ARGBArenaControl listens to the camera zone's
entry and exit events and calls SetArenaAIActive on every live assigned enemy.

While inactive:

- Ground movement and player detection are suppressed.
- Flying pursuit velocity is cleared.
- Shared attack execution and damage timers are canceled.
- Coordinator attack permission is released.
- Teleport timers, active teleport charges, and AoE windups are canceled.
- Teleport and AoE Niagara warning components are deactivated.
- The reported action state is Idle.

The teleporter captures its Blueprint-authored mesh scale during BeginPlay.
Deactivation restores that scale only after it has been captured. This avoids
an actor-initialization-order problem where an arena could deactivate the enemy
first and accidentally replace its authored scale with the temporary default
value of (1,1,1).

When the player enters, enemies resume their appropriate behavior. Teleporters
schedule a new random teleport delay, flyers immediately refresh their player
target, and ground enemies reacquire the player through their detection timer.
When the player exits, the same enemies return to Idle. An enemy that respawns
inherits the arena's current active state, so it cannot begin acting outside an
inactive arena.

Progress-based movement pressure
--------------------------------

The arena can optionally scale enemy movement with puzzle completion. It
linearly blends between Enemy Movement Speed Multiplier At No Progress and
Enemy Movement Speed Multiplier Near Completion whenever platform progress
changes. The resulting multiplier is applied to every active enemy and is also
applied when an enemy registers or returns from respawn.

- Ground enemies multiply their configured Character Movement Max Walk Speed.
- Flying enemies multiply their configured Maximum Flight Speed.
- Teleporters divide their randomized idle interval by the multiplier, because
  their actual relocation is instantaneous rather than velocity-based.

The option is disabled by default. Its default enabled range is 1.0 times speed
at zero progress to 1.5 times speed near completion.

Assignment update timing
------------------------

The coordinator does not run every frame. It uses a repeating timer. The
default AssignmentUpdateInterval is 0.2 seconds. This is frequent enough to
react to the player without requiring group-level logic every Tick.

Selecting attackers
-------------------

With Use Arena Enemy Tuning disabled, the original assignment update is:

1. Invalid or destroyed enemy references are removed.
2. The player is resolved from the stored target or player index 0.
3. Non-teleporter enemies become attack candidates.
4. Candidates are sorted by distance to the player.
5. An enemy already committed as the melee attacker receives priority while
   its commitment is active or while it holds attack permission.
6. The closest candidates are selected up to MaximumSimultaneousAttackers.

The default MaximumSimultaneousAttackers is 1. This is the main mechanism that
prevents every enemy from attacking at exactly the same moment.

Melee slots
-----------

A melee slot is placed to the left or right of the player, based on the
enemy's current side. The default MeleeSlotDistance is 110 cm.

For a ground enemy, the target Z is kept at that enemy's current Z. This avoids
pretending that the current ground implementation can automatically navigate
between platforms.

Pressure slots
--------------

Enemies not selected as attackers receive pressure slots. The slots alternate
between the player's left and right sides. Each pair expands outward in rings.

The defaults are:

- PressureSlotDistance: 280 cm.
- AdditionalPressureSlotSpacing: 140 cm.
- FlyingVerticalSlotSpacing: 140 cm.

Flying pressure slots can also be offset above or below the player. However,
the current flying enemy does not directly fly to its pressure TargetLocation.
It follows the player's live location continuously and reads the coordinator's
assignment only to determine whether it may attack. Therefore the vertical
flying pressure-slot data exists in the coordinator, but it is not yet used as
the flyer's movement destination.

Commitment and assignment leases
--------------------------------

MinimumAssignmentCommitment defaults to 1.5 seconds. When an enemy receives a
new role, the coordinator tries to retain that role for at least this long.
This reduces constant role swapping when two enemies are at almost equal
distances from the player.

AssignmentLeaseDuration defaults to 0.75 seconds. Every update renews the
lease. If updates stop and the lease expires, an enemy treats the assignment as
invalid instead of following stale group data.

Attack permission
-----------------

bCanAttack means the enemy is eligible to ask for permission. It does not mean
damage happens immediately.

When an enemy begins an attack, it calls TryAcquireAttackPermission. The
coordinator counts all currently acquired permissions. If the configured
simultaneous-attacker limit has been reached, the request fails and the enemy
must wait.

Permission is released when:

- The shared melee attack finishes.
- A teleporter's AoE attack resolves.
- An enemy is eliminated.
- An enemy changes coordinator.
- An enemy ends play.
- The arena resets or completes.

Target reservations
-------------------

The coordinator can reserve an AActor for one enemy. Reservations currently
prevent multiple teleporters from selecting the same Target Point during the
same teleport window.

A reservation is rejected when another valid enemy already owns the target.
Invalid enemy and target references are cleaned up during coordinator updates.
Teleporters release their reservation after a successful teleport, a canceled
teleport, or destruction.


5. BASE GROUND ENEMY BEHAVIOR
=============================

Movement plane and CharacterMovement
------------------------------------

Ground enemies use ACharacter and UCharacterMovementComponent. Movement is
constrained to the game's side-view plane:

- X is horizontal gameplay movement.
- Z is vertical world movement.
- Y is the locked depth axis.

The default ground movement values include:

- MaxWalkSpeed: 200 cm/s.
- MaxAcceleration: 2000 cm/s squared.
- BrakingDecelerationWalking: 2000 cm/s squared.
- GravityScale: 3.

The enemy cannot deliberately walk off ledges.

Patrol
------

If patrol is enabled and no coordinated movement takes priority, the ground
enemy moves along X in PatrolDirection.

Before moving, CanWalkInDirection performs two checks:

1. A forward sphere sweep checks for blocking geometry.
2. A downward trace ahead checks that walkable ground exists within the allowed
   step/drop range.

If the path ahead is unsafe, the enemy stops. If the opposite direction is
safe, it begins turning around.

Player detection
----------------

Ground player detection runs on a 0.1-second timer when patrol is enabled.
Detection requires:

- The player to be within DetectionRange.
- The player's feet to be within DetectionHeightTolerance of the enemy's feet.
- An unobstructed visibility trace.

Once detected, LoseInterestRange provides hysteresis so the target is not lost
at the exact detection boundary.

Ground chase and coordinator movement
--------------------------------------

When a valid coordination assignment exists, the ground enemy moves toward the
assignment's X coordinate. It stops within a 10 cm slot tolerance. A selected
attacker then attempts to attack if bCanAttack is true.

If no assignment is available, the base enemy can pursue its locally detected
TargetPlayer directly along X.

Ground enemies do not currently calculate paths between separate platforms.
They stay on their present reachable surface and rely on ledge/wall probes.
Authored platform traversal remains future work.

Turning
-------

When movement direction changes, StartTurn stops movement and tries to play a
TurnMontage. During the montage, the actor rotates over the montage length. If
the required animation setup is missing, the enemy logs a warning and snaps to
the required facing direction instead.


6. SHARED MELEE ATTACK AND DAMAGE PIPELINE
==========================================

Starting an attack
------------------

StartAttackAgainst first calls IsPlayerWithinAttackRange. If the player is in
range, the player becomes TargetPlayer and the shared StartAttack path runs.

An attack cannot start when:

- Another attack is already active.
- The enemy is currently turning.
- The attack cooldown has not expired.
- The arena coordinator refuses attack permission.

Attack timeline
---------------

When an attack begins:

1. Movement stops.
2. The optional AttackMontage plays if one is assigned.
3. The action state becomes Attacking, with no separate preparation timer.
4. A damage timer is scheduled using AttackHitDelay.
5. A finish timer is scheduled using AttackDuration.

AttackDuration is forced to be at least AttackHitDelay plus a small safety
margin. The gameplay timing therefore works even when no animation montage is
assigned. Animation is presentation, not the owner of damage.

Damage resolution
-----------------

At the damage moment, the enemy checks the range again. Damage is applied only
if:

- The target still exists.
- AttackDamage is positive.
- The target is still within the relevant range test.

The enemy then calls ARGBPlayerCharacter::RecieveDamage. The player reduces
CurrentHealth, broadcasts OnHealthChanged, and triggers the existing restart
flow if health reaches zero.

At the end of AttackDuration:

- The attack ends.
- AttackCooldown begins.
- The action state becomes AttackCooldown.
- Coordinator attack permission is released.

Ground attack range
-------------------

Ground enemies compare horizontal X separation using both collision-capsule
radii, StopDistanceFromPlayer, and AttackHitRangePadding. They also compare the
player's feet height with DetectionHeightTolerance. This makes the range test
suitable for two actors standing on approximately the same surface.

Flying attack range
-------------------

Flying enemies override only the range calculation while reusing the rest of
the shared attack pipeline.

The flyer is treated as a sphere based on its capsule radius. The player is
treated as a vertical capsule. The code finds the nearest point on the vertical
center segment of the player's capsule and measures the X/Z distance from the
flyer to that point. The allowed distance includes:

- Flying-enemy capsule radius.
- Player capsule radius.
- StopDistanceFromPlayer.
- AttackHitRangePadding.

This allows a flying enemy to damage the player's body or head. The old
ground-style feet comparison would incorrectly require a flyer to descend to
the player's floor height before an attack could connect.


7. FLYING ENEMY BEHAVIOR
========================

Initialization
--------------

The flying enemy:

- Disables ground patrol.
- Uses a static-mesh FlyingBody for its visible spherical body.
- Hides the inherited skeletal mesh.
- Uses a 48 cm radius and 48 cm half-height capsule by default.
- Disables gravity.
- changes CharacterMovement to MOVE_Flying.
- Locks movement to its initial Y plane.
- Disables CharacterMovement's own component Tick because the class performs a
  controlled swept move from its own actor Tick.
- Refreshes its player reference on a timer, defaulting to every 0.25 seconds.

Player target resolution
------------------------

The flyer first asks Unreal for player character 0. If that fails, it performs
a fallback actor search for ARGBPlayerCharacter. A missing-player warning is
logged once instead of every frame.

Direct pursuit
--------------

Every Tick, the flyer calculates:

    ToTarget = PlayerLocation - EnemyLocation

Y is forced to zero, so pursuit exists only in X and Z. If the target is farther
away than PlayerArrivalRadius, ToTarget becomes the normalized pursuit
direction.

The pursuit direction is weighted by PlayerTrackingWeight and combined with
local separation steering. The default PlayerTrackingWeight is 2.5.

The current flyer follows the player's real position continuously. It does not
enter a traditional idle/patrol loop and does not use the coordinator's
pressure TargetLocation as its flight goal. The coordinator controls whether
the flyer may attack.

Acceleration
------------

The selected clear direction is multiplied by MaximumFlightSpeed. FlightVelocity
approaches that desired velocity using constant interpolation and
FlightAcceleration. When no target or usable direction exists, velocity moves
toward zero using FlightDeceleration.

Defaults:

- MaximumFlightSpeed: 350 cm/s.
- FlightAcceleration: 1200 cm/s squared.
- FlightDeceleration: 900 cm/s squared.
- PlayerArrivalRadius: 10 cm.

Enemy separation
----------------

The flyer performs a local pawn overlap query within EnemySeparationRadius.
For each other ARGBEnemyCharacter, it calculates a direction away from that
enemy. Nearby enemies contribute more strongly than distant ones. The combined
result is clamped and added to player pursuit using EnemySeparationWeight.

Defaults:

- EnemySeparationRadius: 180 cm.
- EnemySeparationWeight: 1.75.

If two enemies occupy exactly the same position, their unique object IDs choose
opposite deterministic X directions. This gives them a way to separate instead
of producing a zero vector.

The flyer's capsule overlaps the Pawn collision channel rather than blocking
it. This prevents physical pushing and stacking forces between enemies and the
player. Separation steering provides behavioral spacing.

Geometry avoidance
------------------

The flyer first probes the direct desired direction. It performs a sphere sweep
on the Pawn collision channel, meaning level geometry that blocks Pawn movement
is treated as an obstacle while overlap-only arena volumes are ignored.

If the direct path is blocked, it evaluates candidate directions:

- Desired direction plus a perpendicular offset.
- Desired direction minus a perpendicular offset.
- Both pure perpendicular directions.
- Straight up.
- Straight down.

Each candidate receives a score based mostly on collision clearance with a
smaller reward for alignment with the desired player direction. The highest
scoring usable direction is selected.

Defaults:

- ObstacleProbeDistance: 240 cm.
- ObstacleProbeRadiusScale: 0.9.
- ObstacleAvoidanceWeight: 2.5.

Swept movement and sliding
--------------------------

The final velocity is converted to a frame movement delta. The movement
component uses SafeMoveUpdatedComponent with sweep enabled. If it hits blocking
geometry, the remaining delta is projected onto the hit surface and a second
swept slide move is attempted. Velocity is projected along the same surface.

This means the flyer does not teleport through geometry during ordinary flight.

Camera-zone confinement
-----------------------

Every registered enemy receives its ARGBArenaCameraZone from ARGBArenaControl.
The flyer also has a one-time fallback search at BeginPlay: if the arena has not
assigned a zone yet, it finds the camera zone containing its spawn position.

Before every primary or slide move, the desired target location is clamped to
the camera zone. The clamp includes padding equal to the flyer's capsule radius
on X/Y and capsule half-height on Z. Therefore the whole collision shape stays
inside the zone, not merely its origin.

If no containing camera zone can be found, the flyer logs a warning and moves
without arena-boundary confinement.

Diagnostics
-----------

If the player is significantly separated on X but the flyer makes almost no X
progress for at least one second, a throttled warning reports:

- Player delta.
- Chosen flight direction.
- Forward clearance.
- The actor hit by the obstacle probe.
- The actor hit by the actual move.

This was added to make collision and axis errors visible in the Output Log.


8. TELEPORTER ENEMY BEHAVIOR
============================

Authored teleport network
-------------------------

With arena tuning enabled, teleporters use the explicit Teleport Target Points
array on the Arena Control. In the legacy mode, they search for
ATargetPoint actors with TeleportPointTag, which defaults to TeleporterPoint.
Only points inside TeleportPointSearchRadius of the teleporter's starting
location are retained.

This authored-point design gives the level designer control over legal,
readable teleport positions.

Random idle timing
------------------

After startup and after every completed or canceled attempt, the teleporter
schedules the next attempt after a random delay between:

- MinimumTeleportIdleTime, default 1.5 seconds.
- MaximumTeleportIdleTime, default 6 seconds.

This removes a perfectly predictable fixed teleport rhythm.

Conditions for attempting a teleport
-------------------------------------

An attempt is postponed if:

- An AoE windup is in progress.
- Another teleport is already in progress.
- The player cannot be found.
- The player is not near any of the teleporter's valid Target Points.

That final proximity gate applies only in legacy mode and uses DetectionRange.
Enabled arena profiles allow teleport movement whenever the encounter is active.

Candidate filtering
-------------------

A Target Point is rejected when:

- It is invalid.
- It was the last successfully used point.
- Another teleporter has reserved it.
- The destination capsule is occupied by another Pawn.
- It is closer than MinimumTeleportDistance to the current location.

Destination Z is raised by the enemy capsule half-height so that a Target Point
placed on the floor represents the enemy's base rather than its center.

Destination scoring
-------------------

For every valid point, the teleporter calculates its distance to the player.
Those distances are normalized from nearest to farthest. Each point then gets:

- A random score.
- A player-closeness score.

FinalScore is an interpolation between those scores using
PlayerProximityWeight. The default weight is 0.65.

At 0, selection is driven by randomness. At 1, selection strongly prefers the
closest valid point. Values in between create a mixture: the teleporter tends
to threaten the player but remains unpredictable.

Reservation and final validation
--------------------------------

The winning Target Point must be reserved before teleport charge begins. When
the charge completes, the destination is recalculated and checked again. This
protects against another Pawn moving into the destination during the warning
period.

If the destination becomes invalid, the charge is canceled, presentation is
reset, the reservation is released, and another random idle period begins.

Teleport presentation
---------------------

During TeleportEffectDelay:

- State becomes TeleportCharge.
- TeleportChargeEffect activates.
- A Niagara user float, normally User.TeleportCharge, moves from 0 to 1.
- TeleportChargeExponent shapes how quickly the visible charge grows.
- After ImplosionStartCharge, the inherited mesh shrinks toward
  ImplosionMinimumScale.
- ImplosionExponent shapes the shrink curve.

At completion, the optional departure effect spawns, TeleportTo performs the
move, the mesh scale is restored, and the optional arrival effect spawns.

Teleporter AoE attack
---------------------

The teleporter checks for an AoE opportunity on a timer. The default interval
is 0.1 seconds.

The attack begins only when:

- No AoE is already winding up.
- No teleport is in progress.
- AoECooldown has expired.
- The player is within AoERadius in the X/Z plane.
- Coordinator attack permission is acquired.

When arena tuning is enabled, attack selection first requires similar feet
heights (Same Level Height Tolerance), then uses Horizontal Attack Range.
Actual damage still requires the X/Z AoE Radius, plus revalidated same-level
and horizontal eligibility. A player on another floor cannot trigger or take
this attack merely by sharing the teleporter's X position.

The warning Niagara component activates during AoEWindupDuration. When the
windup ends, the warning deactivates and attack permission is released. The
player's position is checked again. AoEDamage is applied only if the player is
still inside the radius.

Defaults:

- AoERadius: 250 cm.
- AoEDamage: 1.
- AoEWindupDuration: 0.75 seconds.
- AoECooldown: 2 seconds.
- AoECheckInterval: 0.1 seconds.


9. COLOR, HEALTH, ELIMINATION, AND PLATFORM EFFECTS
==================================================

Enemy color
-----------

Every base enemy owns URGBColorComponent. Its current RGB color determines:

- Which projectile color can damage it.
- Which base and emission materials are assigned.
- Which color a grounded enemy transfers to its supporting platform when it is
  eliminated.

Color-hit validation
--------------------

RecieveColorHit rejects a hit when:

- The enemy has already been eliminated.
- The ColorComponent is invalid.
- The projectile color does not match the enemy's current color.

A matching hit subtracts one health. When health reaches zero:

1. The enemy becomes Disabled.
2. Attack permission is released.
3. Movement stops.
4. A supporting-platform color transfer is attempted.
5. OnEnemyEliminated broadcasts.
6. The actor is destroyed.

Platform color transfer
-----------------------

The base implementation transfers color only when CharacterMovement reports
that the enemy is moving on the ground and the current floor actor is an
ARGBColorPlatform.

Consequences:

- Ground enemies can transfer their color to the platform beneath them.
- A flying enemy normally has no walkable current floor and therefore does not
  transfer color when destroyed in the air.
- A teleporter can transfer color if it is considered grounded on an RGB color
  platform when eliminated.


10. ENEMY RESPAWN AND ARENA LIFECYCLE
=====================================

Spawn records
-------------

ARGBArenaControl records an enemy's class and full spawn transform. Equivalent
class/transform records are reused instead of duplicated.

Elimination handling
--------------------

When OnEnemyEliminated fires, the arena:

- Finds the enemy's spawn record.
- Unregisters it from the coordinator.
- Clears its coordinator and camera-zone references.
- Removes the event binding.
- Removes the actor from live enemy collections.
- Starts a respawn timer if respawning is enabled and the arena is active.

By default, EnemyRespawnDelay supplies one fixed delay and defaults to 5
seconds. A zero delay schedules the respawn for the next Tick rather than
recursively spawning during elimination handling.

The arena can optionally enable Scale Enemy Respawn Delay With Arena Progress.
When enabled, the fixed delay is replaced by a linear blend between Enemy
Respawn Delay At No Progress and Enemy Respawn Delay Near Completion. The
authoritative completion fraction is sampled when the enemy is eliminated, so
an arena with more correctly colored platforms schedules a faster respawn. The
default adaptive range is 8 seconds at 0 percent progress to 2 seconds near
100 percent progress. Completing the arena still cancels every pending respawn.

Respawn
-------

The eliminated actor becomes hidden, non-colliding, inactive, and unavailable
to the coordinator while its respawn timer runs. The actor is restored at its
recorded transform when the timer finishes. Reusing the configured instance
preserves all placed-instance and Blueprint settings, including arena-specific
Teleport Point Tags, flying movement tuning, component setup, and the color
component's Randomize Initial Color option. The color component is reset during
restoration, so enabled randomization selects a new starting color. The arena
then reconnects the actor to:

- Its spawn record.
- The elimination event.
- The camera zone.
- The coordinator.

Arena reset and completion
--------------------------

During reset, pending respawn timers are canceled, coordination is cleared,
and live or waiting enemies are made inactive. The same configured instances
are restored at their recorded transforms when reset finishes.

When the arena completes, pending respawns are canceled, coordination is reset,
and enemies become hidden, non-colliding and inactive without normal color-hit
elimination. Their configured instances are retained so a later arena reset
can restore the same settings even after completion.

12. PERFORMANCE AND RELIABILITY CHARACTERISTICS
================================================

The design avoids several expensive or fragile patterns:

- Group assignment uses a timer instead of per-frame world-wide decision logic.
- Ground detection uses a 0.1-second timer.
- Flying target lookup uses a configurable timer.
- Teleporter AoE checks use a configurable timer.
- Teleport points are discovered once at BeginPlay.
- Flying camera-zone fallback discovery occurs once at BeginPlay.
- UObject references that may disappear are stored as weak pointers where
  appropriate.
- Attack and teleport actions revalidate their targets at the moment of effect.
- Target reservations prevent simultaneous destination selection.
- Assignment leases prevent enemies from trusting stale coordinator data.
- Collision movement uses sweeps rather than unswept location changes, except
  for the deliberate validated TeleportTo action.


13. CURRENT LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE EXTENSIONS
=============================================

The following points describe the present implementation accurately and should
not be presented as completed features:

1. There is no Behavior Tree, Blackboard, EQS query, or NavMesh-based planner.

2. Ground enemies cannot navigate between separated platforms. They patrol and
   pursue only on locally reachable ground.

3. The planned puzzle-aware sabotage system is not implemented yet. Enemies do
   not currently score platform colors or choose actions based on predicted
   arena progress.

4. Cannon-aware intentional sacrifice behavior remains future work.

5. Flying pressure-slot target locations are calculated by the coordinator but
   are not yet used for flight movement. Flyers track the player's live
   position and use coordination for attack permission.

6. Flying obstacle avoidance is local steering. It does not build a complete
   path around complex mazes and can become trapped in geometry that requires a
   long detour beyond its probe choices.

7. Enemy separation is a steering influence, not a guaranteed formation
   solver. Tight spaces can still compress enemies.

8. Teleporter destination occupancy checks use Pawn overlap. Level designers
   must place Target Points at valid floor positions and keep their surrounding
   geometry suitable for the capsule.

9. Flying enemies normally do not transfer color to platforms because they are
   not grounded when eliminated.

10. The shared system is designed for local single-player play. Networking and
    authority replication have not been implemented.


14. SUGGESTED PRESENTATION STRUCTURE
====================================

The system can be presented clearly in the following order:

Slide 1: Design goal
    Explain that multiple enemy types should pressure the player without all
    selecting the same position or attacking simultaneously.

Slide 2: Two-level architecture
    Show the arena coordinator above several autonomous enemy actors. Explain
    that the coordinator manages shared resources while each enemy owns its
    local movement and attack execution.

Slide 3: Shared state vocabulary
    Present the action states and the three coordination roles.

Slide 4: Attacker and pressure selection
    Explain distance sorting, commitment time, melee slots, pressure slots, and
    the simultaneous-attacker limit.

Slide 5: Ground enemy
    Show random patrol, ledge/wall probing, visible-player chase and speed boost,
    immediate attack animation, configured damage moment, and cooldown.

Slide 6: Teleporter enemy
    Show authored points, candidate rejection, weighted randomness, reservation,
    charge, teleport, and AoE warning.

Slide 7: Flying enemy
    Show player pursuit plus separation plus obstacle avoidance, followed by
    swept movement and camera-zone clamping.

Slide 8: Combat and color integration
    Explain matching-color damage, health, elimination, platform transfer, and
    the difference between grounded and flying elimination.

Slide 9: Arena lifecycle
    Explain registration, elimination events, delayed respawn, reset, and arena
    completion cleanup.

Slide 10: Considered reinforcement-learning workflow
    Explain that Unreal-MAP was investigated as a possible multi-agent
    reinforcement-learning workflow. Describe its potential for automated
    simulation, learned cooperation, and AI evaluation, followed by the scope
    reasons for retaining the existing deterministic C++ architecture.

Slide 11: Strengths and limitations
    Present the system as an explicit, debuggable C++ foundation and identify
    platform traversal, puzzle-aware decisions, and advanced path planning as
    future development.


15. INVESTIGATED UNREAL-MAP WORKFLOW AND SCOPE DECISION
=======================================================

Unreal-MAP repository:
https://github.com/binary-husky/unreal-map

Unreal-MAP is a research platform for multi-agent reinforcement learning in
Unreal Engine. It provides a workflow in which Unreal supplies a simulated
environment while an external Python system observes agents, selects actions,
assigns rewards, and repeats encounters across many training episodes. This
could potentially be used to train cooperation between different enemy types,
run automated arena simulations, compare AI strategies, and gather balancing
data without manually replaying every encounter.

The workflow was investigated as a possible extension for RGB. A suitable RGB
training interface would need to expose observations such as the player's
position, velocity, health and color; enemy positions, states and cooldowns;
platform colors; arena boundaries; and nearby obstacles. It would also need to
define a controlled set of actions such as moving, holding distance, attacking,
teleporting, retreating, or remaining idle. Reward rules would then be required
for useful pressure, damage, spacing, survival, collision avoidance, and
remaining inside the active arena.

This approach was not adopted during the project because its implementation
scope became too large relative to how far development had already progressed.
Unreal-MAP is presented as a complete research environment rather than a small
enemy-AI plugin. Its documented setup targets a source-built Unreal Engine 4.27
environment, while RGB uses Unreal Engine 5.7. Adopting it would therefore have
required engine-version migration work, an external Python training pipeline,
new communication and episode-reset interfaces, additional dependencies, and
a redesign of parts of the established C++ enemy architecture. Training,
evaluating, and converting a learned policy into reliable game behavior would
also require substantial time beyond the integration itself.

RGB already had a functioning deterministic C++ architecture with explicit
enemy states, arena ownership, attack permissions, destination reservations,
respawning, and specialized movement for each enemy type. Continuing with that
architecture kept the behavior debuggable, directly tunable in Unreal, and
achievable within the project schedule. Unreal-MAP was therefore retained as a
researched alternative and possible future-work direction rather than being
integrated late in development.

Presentation-ready slide text
-----------------------------

I investigated Unreal-MAP as a possible workflow for training coordinated
enemy behavior through multi-agent reinforcement learning. It could support
automated simulations, learned cooperation, and AI evaluation. However,
adopting it would require a separate Python training pipeline, custom
environment interfaces, additional dependencies, and considerable migration
work from Unreal Engine 4.27 to 5.7. Because the project already had an
established C++ AI architecture, the integration exceeded the available scope.
I therefore continued with a deterministic, state-based multi-agent
coordination system.

Short presentation bullets
--------------------------

- Investigated Unreal-MAP for multi-agent reinforcement learning.
- Potential benefits included automated training, coordination, and testing.
- Required a Python training pipeline and major Unreal-version integration.
- RGB already had substantial C++ AI infrastructure.
- Chose deterministic state-based coordination to keep the project achievable.
- Retained reinforcement learning as possible future work.

Suggested speaking note
-----------------------

Unreal-MAP was technically relevant, but introducing it late would have meant
rebuilding much of the existing AI around an external training workflow. I
treated it as useful future work rather than risking the stability and
completion of the current project.


16. SHORT SUMMARY FOR SPEAKING NOTES
====================================

RGB uses an arena-scoped multi-agent coordinator combined with autonomous enemy
actors. The coordinator regularly observes the player and registered enemies,
selects a limited number of attackers, assigns other enemies to pressure roles,
and manages shared attack permissions and destination reservations. Each enemy
variant then performs its own specialized behavior. Ground enemies patrol and
use local collision probes, teleporters choose among authored points using a
blend of player proximity and randomness, and flying enemies pursue in the X/Z
plane using separation, obstacle avoidance, swept movement, and camera-zone
confinement. Attacks are timer-driven and revalidate range at the damage moment.
The arena owns enemy registration, elimination tracking, delayed respawn, reset,
and completion cleanup. This creates a modular C++ base that can later grow into
puzzle-aware sabotage, authored platform traversal, and cannon-aware tactics.
